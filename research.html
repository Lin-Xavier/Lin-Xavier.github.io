Research

My interests are experimental psycholinguistics (especially artificial language learning), language evolution, language processing, efficient communication and the law of abbreviation, proper names and compositionality, and onomastics.

Projects

  • 2026

    Does word class matter when speakers abbreviate? Evidence from sentence completion and dyadic communication

    MSc dissertation, University of Edinburgh

    I tested how word class, frequency and the pressure to communicate affect abbreviation. I ran artificial language experiments in English and Chinese, and an English communication game played in pairs. I built the experiments in jsPsych with a Python WebSocket server for real-time play, and analysed the data in R with logistic and mixed-effects regression. People were reluctant to shorten place-names, but personal names showed no reliable effect of the same kind.

  • 2025

    From Cantonese Alleyways to Imperial Avenues: Onomastics in Hong Kong's Street-Names

    BA dissertation, University of Nottingham

    I built a corpus of 4,585 official Hong Kong street-names with their English and Chinese forms, and archived it openly on OSF. I adapted the Australian National Placenames Survey typology to a bilingual setting. The English and Chinese generic terms line up systematically, but not one to one. I also proposed "two-character stitching" as a word-formation pattern to test in experiments. An essay from this work was a joint winner of the Society for Name Studies in Britain and Ireland Essay Prize in 2025.

  • 2024

    Usage labels in early nineteenth-century German-English dictionaries

    Research Assistant, University of Nottingham

    I studied evaluative usage labels in bilingual dictionaries. I chose the source dictionaries, designed the sampling method, and combined frequency analysis with close reading of selected lemmas. I presented the results as a poster, which won the runner-up presentation prize.
